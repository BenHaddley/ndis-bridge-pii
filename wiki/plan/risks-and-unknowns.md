# Open risks and unknowns

- **9820's RNDIS behaviour is unproven.** RNDIS is a spec, and implementations can have device-specific quirks (link-state reporting, MTU, throughput ceilings). The specification defines what correct behaviour looks like — the initialize handshake and its `MaxTransferSize` ([`MS-RNDIS.pdf`](../../references/pdf/MS-RNDIS.pdf) §2.2.2 p. 12, §2.2.9 p. 18) and media connect/disconnect signalling (§2.2.7 p. 16) — but having the document does not tell you whether this device honours it. Nothing here substitutes for plugging the real device into real Linux and watching what `dmesg` says.
- **USB power budget.** The Pi 4 limits downstream USB current to roughly 1.1 A in aggregate across its four sockets ([Pi 4 datasheet](../../references/pdf/raspberry-pi-4-datasheet.pdf) §5.3 p. 11), and wants a 5 V 3 A supply itself (§4.1 p. 8). An underfed bus shows up as resets and disappearing interfaces, not as an obvious power error.
- **The radio link may not carry Layer 2 — and this is the biggest open risk in the project.** If the [Harris data link](../concepts/transport-link.md) presents a routed Layer-3 interface rather than a transparent Ethernet bridge, the flat-LAN design stops at the radio, broadcast discovery does not cross it, and routing becomes the primary architecture. Establish this from the radio's documentation before building anything.
- **Radio link throughput may not carry the video at all.** Tactical data links can be one to three orders of magnitude slower than the Wi-Fi link this design originally assumed. The [32 Mbit/s planning example](../test/throughput-and-acceptance.md) is a Wi-Fi-era figure and should be treated as void until a measured radio figure replaces it. If the gap is large, the fix is a different video design, not network tuning.
- **In-path router defeats the bridge.** Putting a router between the switch and the Pi creates a routed hop inside what was supposed to be one broadcast domain. Keep the router upstream-only unless routing is the deliberate design — see [Switch and router selection](../build/switch-and-router.md).
- **No IGMP querier on an isolated segment.** With no router on the camera VLAN, multicast group memberships expire and streams stall a minute or two in. The switch must be configured as querier.
- **Multiple DHCP servers.** Covered in [DHCP: pick exactly one server](dhcp.md), but worth repeating as a risk: any combination of the 9820, radio, router and Pi serving DHCP is a common source of hard-to-diagnose bugs if more than one is left enabled.
- **9820 identity still unconfirmed.** The topology diagram names it a **Garmin 9820**, which is more than the wiki had before, but it is still an assumption rather than a recorded fact. If it is a Garmin marine unit, check whether it offers a native Ethernet marine-network port — if it does, the entire USB/RNDIS approach may be unnecessary. Confirm before building.
- **Multicast/broadcast dependence, if routing is chosen instead of bridging.** If the video/discovery protocol relies on multicast or broadcast (very common for camera discovery protocols), [Option 2 (routing)](../concepts/bridge-vs-route.md#option-2-layer-3-routing) needs protocol-specific relaying or multicast routing; an IGMP proxy does not relay arbitrary broadcast discovery that [Option 1 (bridging)](../concepts/bridge-vs-route.md#option-1-layer-2-bridge-try-this-first) avoids by construction.
- **VM results don't fully transfer to the Pi.** A working ARM64 VM test proves the Linux networking approach is sound, not that the Pi's specific USB/Ethernet silicon will behave identically. See [Testing without a Pi](../test/vm-testing.md).
